### About the project

Atmospheric gravity waves transport energy and momentum through
the atmosphere, playing an important role in determining its
large-scale circulation and variability. However, their relatively
small spatial scales make them particularly difficult to observe.

Even our most advanced satellite instruments detect only part of
the gravity wave spectrum. Different observing systems are
sensitive to different wavelengths, atmospheric altitudes and
wave characteristics. As a result, no individual instrument can
provide a complete picture of global gravity wave activity.

**Seeing the Unseen aims to overcome these observational
limitations by combining measurements from complementary
instruments into a more complete description of the global
gravity wave field.**

### Research approach

The project brings together satellite remote sensing,
ground-based observations and high-resolution atmospheric
modelling. By characterising how individual instruments measure
and distort gravity waves, we aim to combine their observations
in a physically consistent way.

This will allow us to investigate the global transport of
momentum by gravity waves more comprehensively, improving the
observational constraints available for understanding atmospheric
dynamics and evaluating numerical models.

An additional research strand investigates the relationship
between stratospheric gravity wave activity and tropical cyclone
intensification, including whether these measurements could
provide useful information about developing intense storms.
