I am Professor of Atmospheric Remote Sensing at the University of Bath, where I lead research into the dynamics of the Earth's atmosphere, particularly atmospheric gravity waves and their role in weather, climate and the circulation of the middle atmosphere.

My research combines satellite observations, numerical modelling and data analysis to understand atmospheric processes across a wide range of spatial and temporal scales. I am particularly interested in developing new methods to extract information from satellite measurements, and in using these observations to improve our understanding of atmospheric dynamics and the performance of numerical models.

I have worked at Bath since 2013, following research positions at the National Center for Atmospheric Research in the USA and the Université de Bretagne Occidentale in France. I previously held a Royal Society University Research Fellowship and have led or contributed to research projects funded by UKRI, NERC, the Royal Society, the Leverhulme Trust and international partners.

Alongside my research, I co-lead the international SPARC Gravity Waves Activity and contribute to several international satellite mission and atmospheric science programmes. I also supervise doctoral researchers, teach atmospheric and space science, and have previously served as Director of Research in Bath's Department of Electronic and Electrical Engineering and as founding Co-Director of its Centre for Climate Adaptation and Environment Research.
